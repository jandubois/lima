Assessment ¶

62 failed jobs against 129 the week before, on 3,569 jobs run against 4,278. The failure rate fell from 3.0% to 1.7% against a 3.4% four-week average, so the failures fell faster than the volume did.

master is where nothing improved. Its 14 failures match the previous week exactly and sit above the 10.8 four-week average, so the whole drop belongs to PR branches. Every one of the 14 has a verdict and none is a regression: thirteen are known flakes or a recorded one-off, and the fourteenth is a log that expired before anyone read it.

Twenty-three of the 62 are proven flakes, where the identical job passed on a re-run of the same commit. More than a third of the week's failures cost a developer a re-run and told them nothing. requirement-script-bad-fd is the one moving the wrong way: 10 jobs against a 6.8 four-week average, its highest in six weeks (4, 6, 5, 8, 8, 10), and 3 of them on master. It reports a guest requirement as unsatisfied when the requirement script never ran at all, so the instance is declared degraded and the message names the wrong cause. Root-cause that one; re-running it only hides it. lychee-link-unreachable is larger at 14 but flat across the same six weeks (16, 13, 20, 21, 12, 14), and it is lima's own docs site refusing connections, so it says nothing about the code.

Five flakes now have fixes in open PRs, and two of them fired this week: #5355 ignores SIGPIPE in test-port-forwarding.pl, and #5356 waits for the coredns forward before querying it, between them accounting for 3 of the week's failures. The other three stayed silent, which tells us nothing about their fixes while those sit unmerged. Nothing new needs a matcher: the only unrecognized surface appearing on more than one branch is the socat port-forwarding message, and one of those two jobs was read and recorded as a one-off.

How this report is built

ci-sweep weekly --week 2026-W30 over sweep.db, one row per failed job — a run can fail on one matrix leg out of many, so the job is the unit.

  • Known flake — a job whose log matched a matchers.yaml pattern. Pattern matching only, no LLM.
  • One flake per job — when several matchers fire, the job counts under the one whose surface appears earliest in the log, because the first failure is the root and what follows is fallout: a suite killed at its wall-clock cap logs a failed wait on the way out. The others are recorded but not counted, and only a true tie is left uncounted.
  • Proven flake — the identical job passed on a re-run of the same commit, so the code cannot be at fault.
  • Unrecognized — no matcher fired. On master it earns analysis; on a PR branch it earns only a count, because PR failures are dev breakage, not CI health.
  • No log — the log expired or was never uploaded, so the sweep could not classify the failure. A data gap, not a clean bill.
  • What failed — for a known flake, the matcher's summary; otherwise the failing test and command, or the job error for a failure that never reached its tests. --fetch names the rest, at one download each.
  • Counts cover only jobs the sweep recorded. A period never swept reads as zero failures.
