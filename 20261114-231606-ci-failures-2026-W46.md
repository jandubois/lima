## Assessment

The failure rate rose to 4.5% from 3.1% on fewer jobs (2,175 against 3,559), and 29 of the 98 failures came from MSYS2 and Arch Linux rebuilding their tools against OpenSSL 4. A partial upgrade kept OpenSSL 3 on the Windows runners (18 jobs) and in the Arch guests (11, 2 on `master`) until #5780 and #5788 made both upgrades full on 11-10, and neither failure has come back since. 16 of the week's 17 `instance-start-timeout` jobs started between 11-09 22:56Z and 11-10 06:51Z, 11 of them in one `msys2-full-upgrade` run across Linux, macOS and Windows jobs. Those guests booted and accepted ssh but never finished installing sshfs or containerd, and 14 of the 17 passed on a re-run. So the cause was probably outside lima, such as a slow package mirror, though the job logs don't show the guests' own output. `vz-exfat-mount-after-mkfs` fell to 4 of 60 vz `default.yaml` jobs from 14 of 97, but at a constant rate 4 or fewer would happen about 11% of the time, so the drop doesn't show a fix. The cause stays unknown until `bats::on_failure` in `hack/bats/extras/vz-block-device.bats` captures the guest's `dmesg`. The one regression candidate, 129681401104, is the known hdiutil eject failure at `chown_darwin_test.go:66` ("Resource busy"), which #5354's `-force` doesn't cover, and `TestChownIntegration` still detaches without a retry.

<summary>How this report is built</summary>

`ci-sweep weekly --week 2026-W46` over `sweep.db`, one row per **failed job** — a run can fail on one matrix leg out of many, so the job is the unit.

- **Known flake** — a job whose log matched a `matchers.yaml` pattern. Pattern matching only, no LLM.
- **One flake per job** — when several matchers fire, the job counts under the one whose surface appears **earliest** in the log, because the first failure is the root and what follows is fallout: a suite killed at its wall-clock cap logs a failed wait on the way out. The others are recorded but not counted, and only a true tie is left uncounted.
- **Proven flake** — the identical job passed on a re-run of the same commit, so the code cannot be at fault.
- **Unrecognized** — no matcher fired. On `master` it earns analysis; on a PR branch it earns only a count, because PR failures are dev breakage, not CI health.
- **No log** — the log expired or was never uploaded, so the sweep could not classify the failure. A data gap, not a clean bill.
- **What failed** — for a known flake, the matcher's summary; otherwise the failing test and command, or the job error for a failure that never reached its tests. `--fetch` names the rest, at one download each.
- Counts cover only jobs the sweep recorded. A period never swept reads as zero failures.

</details>
