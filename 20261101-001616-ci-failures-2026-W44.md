## Assessment

The failure rate fell to 2.3% from 3.4%, but #5486, merged on 10-29, brought the week's one real regression. After the merge `VMNet tests (QEMU, macos-15-large)` failed 18 of its 27 runs, 2 of 4 on `master`, and all 18 lost their runner in the new `Run BATS shared/host network tests` step, while the `ubuntu-24.04` job with the same step passed 21 of 21. One commit lost the runner three times in a row and another lost it twice and then passed, so a green re-run doesn't clear a commit here; the three of these jobs under Proven flakes still belong to the regression. In 15 of the 18 the runner went silent about three minutes into the step, while `setup_file` was starting a `lima:shared` and then a `lima:host` instance, but I don't know the mechanism and nobody has filed the regression. So the next step is to run variants of the step on a fork and separate `lima:host`, which had never run on macOS CI, from the second VM. The flake to root-cause is `vz-exfat-mount-after-mkfs`, which rose to 11 jobs from 2 (4 on `master`) and has only appeared since the Ubuntu 26.10 update (#5657); nothing in the logs says why the mount fails, so `bats::on_failure` needs to capture the guest's `dmesg` and `blkid` output first.

<summary>How this report is built</summary>

`ci-sweep weekly --week 2026-W44` over `sweep.db`, one row per **failed job** — a run can fail on one matrix leg out of many, so the job is the unit.

- **Known flake** — a job whose log matched a `matchers.yaml` pattern. Pattern matching only, no LLM.
- **One flake per job** — when several matchers fire, the job counts under the one whose surface appears **earliest** in the log, because the first failure is the root and what follows is fallout: a suite killed at its wall-clock cap logs a failed wait on the way out. The others are recorded but not counted, and only a true tie is left uncounted.
- **Proven flake** — the identical job passed on a re-run of the same commit, so the code cannot be at fault.
- **Unrecognized** — no matcher fired. On `master` it earns analysis; on a PR branch it earns only a count, because PR failures are dev breakage, not CI health.
- **No log** — the log expired or was never uploaded, so the sweep could not classify the failure. A data gap, not a clean bill.
- **What failed** — for a known flake, the matcher's summary; otherwise the failing test and command, or the job error for a failure that never reached its tests. `--fetch` names the rest, at one download each.
- Counts cover only jobs the sweep recorded. A period never swept reads as zero failures.

</details>
